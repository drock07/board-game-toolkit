"When a player plays a +2, anyone may stack another +2 on it." "When a monster
enters your space, you may attack." Reactions interrupt whatever is going on,
then hand control back. In the toolkit, that's a **trigger**, often with a
**race** for who responds.

[Plus Two](/board-game-toolkit/examples/plus-two/) builds a stacking response window.

## The trigger

<Source file="games/plus-two/spec.ts" />

- `on` watches for a card moving from any hand to the discard pile.
- `when: "plusTwoPlayed"` narrows it to +2 cards. Inside it,
  `scope.event` is the move.
- `flow` runs as an **interrupt** on top of whatever made the move: the turn
  decision, or another window.

## The window

The window's locals work out, from the event, who stacked and who's next. Its
lists give the actors for each side of the race:

<Source file="games/plus-two/impl.ts" region="window" />

The `parallel` with `join: "race"` gives every other player a `stack` prompt
and the victim a `takePenalty` prompt at once. Whoever answers first ends the
race; the other branch is cancelled.

## Nesting

Stacking moves a +2 from a hand to the discard pile, which fires the trigger
again, inside the current window. The new window interrupts the old one,
whose `accept` is then rejected ("The penalty has moved on") because its
victim no longer matches. When the innermost window resolves, the outer ones
unwind in turn, and the original turn carries on.

## Tips

- Keep `when` conditions cheap and specific: they run for every matching event.
- Use `priority` when several triggers can fire from one event, and
  `triggerOrder` to choose FIFO or LIFO for events from the same step.
- A trigger flow can raise an outcome that unwinds through the interrupted
  flow, ending a turn early, for example.
- Watch flow events, `{ type: "flow", kind: "enter", node: "turn" }`, for
  "at the start of your turn" abilities.
