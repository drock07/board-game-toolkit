Most nodes end normally: a `seq` runs out of children, a prompt gets its
answer. Games also need to stop things early. You bust, so your turn and the
dealer's are skipped; the boss falls mid-fight, so the whole run ends in
victory. That's what **outcomes** are for.

`outcomes(outcomes, body)` runs its body and lists the ways the body may end
early. Each outcome has a name and, optionally:

- **`when`**: a guard, a condition that ends the body as soon as it holds.
- **`then`**: a node to run after the body is cancelled.

When an outcome happens, everything running inside the body is cancelled,
`then` runs if there is one, and the `outcomes` node ends. The flow carries on
after it.

## Guards

**Guards are checked after every transaction**, not just between nodes. In
the demo, a roll that takes the total over 10 ends the push-your-luck loop on
the spot, even though the loop never finishes on its own.

Guards are checked **outermost first**. If two hold at once, the one on the
outer node wins. Dungeon Crawl relies on this: killing the dragon also kills
the monster in the room, and the run's outer `victory` beats the fight's own
`killed`.

## Raising an outcome

An action's `execute` or a step may return `{ exit: "name" }` to raise an
outcome by name. It unwinds to the nearest enclosing `outcomes` node that
lists that name. An outcome doesn't need a `when` to be raised; Dungeon
Crawl's `fled` has none:

Raising an outcome nothing handles is a bug in the game: it throws
`UnhandledOutcomeError`.

## While the handler runs

While an outcome's `then` runs, its `outcomes` node checks no guards and
catches nothing, so an outcome raised by the handler unwinds past it to the
next one out.

## Outcomes and the rest of the flow

- **Fibers.** An outcome that escapes one player's fiber in a `simultaneous`
  cancels the others too, and carries on in the parent.
- **Reactions.** If an effect's abilities are still running when a guard
  fires, they finish first, and then the outcome's handler runs. What
  happened still gets its reactions. See
  [Effects and abilities](/board-game-toolkit/concepts/effects/).
- **Tests.** There's no "outcome" event; an outcome shows in what it does.
  Assert on that: the vars a handler sets, a waiting label, a zone's contents.

for a live demo.
