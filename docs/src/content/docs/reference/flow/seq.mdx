`seq` is "this, then this": a round that deals, plays and scores, or a game
that sets up, plays and tallies. You write `seq(...children)`.

| Argument   | Type     | What it is                  |
| ---------- | -------- | --------------------------- |
| `children` | `Node[]` | The nodes to run, in order. |

## Demo

A round in four parts. The three steps run straight through; only the
prompt waits for you.

<Demo name="seq" />

## Behavior

- **One at a time.** Each child starts when the one before it ends. A child
  that waits, like a `prompt`, holds the `seq` there until it's answered.
- **Ends with its last child.** A `seq` with no children ends at once.
- **No state of its own.** A `seq` doesn't bind a player or show anything;
  it's a list.

A `seq` is also the usual body for something that takes options, like a
turn of several parts inside `turns`. Plus Two's turn is a play, then a
reaction window, every turn:

<Source file="games/plus-two/game.ts" region="rules" />

To end a `seq` partway, when the player busts or the boss falls, wrap it
in [`outcomes`](/board-game-toolkit/reference/flow/outcomes/).
