`anyone` is a race or an interruption: whoever grabs the coin first,
anyone who wants to stack a +2, a player calling "Uno!". It waits on several
players at once and ends at the first answer. You write
`anyone(opts, ...actions)`.

| Option    | Type                | Default      | What it is                                                  |
| --------- | ------------------- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `who`     | `(s) => PlayerId[]` | Every player | Who it waits on. Read whenever the engine asks who may act. |
| `label`   | `string`            |              | What players are told it's waiting for. In `view.waiting`.  |
| `actions` | handles             |              | The actions any of them may take.                           |

## Who and legality

`anyone` offers every action to everyone in `who`. Which of them may
actually take which action is up to the actions themselves: their
`validate` gets the `actor`, and returns a reason when that player can't.

Plus Two's reaction window waits on everyone but the last stacker. Any of
them may stack a +2 they hold; only the current victim may take the
penalty:

<Source file="games/plus-two/game.ts" region="accept" />

This split keeps hands hidden. **Everyone in `who` is an actor**, in
`actors()` and in `view.waiting`, whether or not they have a legal input
right now. So decide `who` from public facts, never from hidden cards: a
`who` of "players holding a +2" would tell the table who holds one.

## Behavior

- **First answer wins.** The first legal input from any of them ends the
  `anyone`. Returning `{ exit: "name" }` raises an outcome instead.
- **One entry in `view.waiting`**, with all of `who` as its `actors`.
- **An empty `who` waits on no one.** It doesn't end: the flow is stuck
  there, with no actors and no legal inputs. Make sure `who` can't be
  empty, or skip the `anyone` with a `branch` when it would be.
